Signature Assignment · 400 points · Due Nov 19

The Field Log and Its Double

A real experiment in seeing. You log a lived encounter, generate a machine account of the same thing, and study the seam between them — the border of what the machine cannot cross.

The premise

This is the heart of Encountering the World. You will do a real observation, keep an empirical log of a lived encounter, generate an AI account of the same phenomenon, and analyze the gap through the frameworks of the course. You will document your AI use and adapt the method to your own field. The seam between your account and the machine's is where the learning happens.

The primary record must reflect your direct observations — because the computational double can only be evaluated against an account verified by human perception.

Why the order matters

Your analytical argument must be finalized before technical construction, so the final build stays accountable to your own theoretical framework. Weekly logs and a mid-term essay keep your interpretive process transparent — a deliberate revision from an earlier version of this course that collected everything only at the end.

What you will do

  1. Choose your site or phenomenon

    Select one site, object, or phenomenon in a field you care about — something you can return to repeatedly across the term and observe with your own senses.

  2. Keep the Field Log

    Across the weeks, maintain an empirical observational log. Prioritize rigorous, objective description over interpretation. Submit entries weekly so your process stays visible and assessable.

  3. Write the Critical Encounter Essay

    By the mid-term, finalize your analytical argument about the phenomenon, drawing on course frameworks — situated knowledge, borderlands thinking, Actor-Network Theory, critical AI studies. This argument is fixed before you generate the machine's account.

  4. Generate the Machine's Double

    Prompt a computational model to construct its own account of the same site using your compiled field notes. Keep every substantive prompt and output.

  5. Analyze the seam

    Compare the two accounts. Name what the model reproduces accurately, what it homogenizes, and what its fluency obscures. Read the gap through your frameworks — this is the heart of the assignment.

  6. Plan the transfer

    Adapt the method to your own disciplinary or teaching context: how would this critical practice travel to the students and tools you will actually work with?

  7. Disclose your AI use

    Submit a process note: the tools used, the queries run, and the outputs you kept or rejected, with reflection on at least one bias or limit you encountered.

How it is assessed

The analysis is the skill being assessed — not the polish of the artifact and not the fluency of the machine's prose. Strong dossiers show rigorous description in the field log, an argument that was genuinely settled before the build, and a comparative analysis that names precisely what the machine homogenized or obscured. Transparent, thoughtful AI disclosure is graded as a formal component: the point is explicit methodology, not surveillance.

Frameworks to bring

Situated knowledge (Haraway) · borderlands and nepantla (Anzaldúa) · Actor-Network Theory (Latour) · missing datasets (Onuoha) · the politics of AI infrastructure (Crawford, Benjamin, Noble) · attention and embodied knowing (Kimmerer).
